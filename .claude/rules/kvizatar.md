# KvizAtar (id `osvajanje`, folders `bitka/`)

**KvizAtar** (id is still `osvajanje`, folders are `bitka/` — the game was renamed after launch and renaming an id would mean migrating saved timings and host settings) — Triviador/Konquiztador: 2–4 players, one castle each, questions decide who expands and who takes whose land. **How many players fit is a property of the map, not a constant**: the strict map check stays at `BITKA_MIN_TERITORIJA` (9) so a small map remains valid for two, and `validateStart` refuses the *start* when the chosen map has fewer than `bitkaMinTeritorija(players)` (3 per player — one castle + two to conquer) or fewer marked castle sites than players. Without that, the last player in the placement queue can end up with no site at all. The no-map-chosen fallback (`firstValidBitkaMapIdSync`) takes a minimum-territory hint so a four-player table doesn't default to the alphabetically-first tiny map. **Length is a game-select choice** (`bitkaMode`, deliberately *not* `GAME_ROUND_CONFIG` — that selector is a bare round count and can't express "until the last castle"): `zamkovi` runs the war until one castle stands (~25 min; `BITKA_MAX_RATNIH_RUNDI` stays a stuck-game guard, not a setting), `runde` plays `BITKA_RUNDE_IZBOR` (6/9/12) rounds and then ranks by score. **Turns always rotate — with one exception**: while a besieger keeps breaking walls they hold the turn and get a fresh question against the *same* castle (`duel-rezultat` in the module). That chain is self-limiting (three walls) and never idles anyone, since both duellists are on their phones; a general "attack again on any win" rule was considered and rejected because it lets one player run the board while the others watch. In a two-player game that chain can end the match before the opponent moves — kept, but stated plainly in `/uputstva`. **A war round ends when the turn pointer wraps back to where the round started** (`roundStartPointer`), never on an attack count: counting attacks against `alive.length` taken *after* an elimination let a round close with the last player never having attacked. Each round starts one seat further along (`beginRound`), so the same player doesn't open every round. **A disconnected player never holds the table**: their `napad-izbor` is skipped on the spot ("nije tu — potez ide dalje") and their castle/land picks auto-resolve immediately instead of running the 14/8 s timers. `IZBOR_DURATION` is 8 s (was 12 — an adjacent pick is usually two or three options). **`kraj` is the final beat** (`KRAJ_DURATION`, tunable, 6 s): same board *not* remounted (so the `pobeda` confetti actually plays on it), an Objava with the winner and the sentence that ended the war (`lastEvent`), leaderboard with eliminated/removed players ranked **below** every survivor regardless of score — note the platform's own final screen still ranks by `player.score`, documented in `finishGame`. Scoring is made visible instead of assumed: `scoringLegend` rides `hostData` during `uvod` only, land messages carry the points ("uzima Porodin (+200)"), and `duel.scoreDeltas` goes out with `pendingOutcome` so `duel-ishod` can say "Pera +200 · Mika −200" (the commit pass warns if the real change differs). `poolWarning` (also `uvod` only) says when the chosen packs hold fewer questions than the match will draw, because the pool otherwise recycles silently. On the TV the `duelArena` layout became a general **question arena** for every answering and reveal phase (the 4.4 rem strip under a 76 vh map was unreadable from a couch), the pick-phase Objava sits at the bottom edge with a light dim so the offered territories stay visible, and the 3D board shows the owner's emoji over each castle. On the phone a tap on a non-offered territory is answered (red outline, haptic, "Nije susedna tvojoj zemlji") with a 22 px fat-finger fallback to the nearest offered one, every answer screen shares one `Clock` header and one `SubmittedBar`, and an eliminated player keeps the normal spectator screen with a "🏳️ Ispao si" chip instead of a bare map. **Maps are content, not code**: drawn in `/admin` → Mape over an uploaded PNG and stored in `bitka-maps/<id>.json` (+ `<id>/` holding the image, served at `/bitka-files/<id>/<file>`). Maps are *illustrations*, so there is **no geography** — no lat/lng, no bbox, no `serbia-projection`; every coordinate is normalized `{x,y} ∈ [0,1]` over the image, the same space the kviz geo pins use. Questions come from the ordinary kviz packs via the shared `quizPackIds` field (obicno/uljez/**matrica** for the land grab and duels, broj for the tiebreaker; `pogodi-broj` is the fallback and, failing that, speed decides). **Eight types are playable** — `BITKA_PITANJE_TYPES` = obicno/uljez/matrica/redosled/domino/emoji/dopuna/anagram, plus `broj` for the tiebreak (`BITKA_QUIZ_TYPES`). Only three kviz types are refused: audio/video (media — the TV already carries the board and a hostless room has nowhere to play it), geo (a second map, when the map *is* the battlefield) and piksel (needs the progressive de-pixelation pipeline). **Every type plays in both places** — the land grab and the duel draw from one pool, so "add a type" never means "add a phase". The text types (emoji/dopuna/anagram) carry a known, accepted cost: typing puts thumb speed into the outcome, and a miss may be retried until the clock runs out (a typo must not cost a castle, and the checker already tolerates small ones), so a duel on them usually runs the full 20 s instead of ending the moment both answer. They are worth checking on deliberately rather than leaving every type on. Anagram is **not** progressively unscrambled the way kviz does it — a 20 s duel does not need a fourth timing state or another surface for the answer to leak through, so `scrambleOf` shuffles once at `setQuestion`. Domino is the one type that isn't a single move: the player walks the chain and stops at the first miss, so `answers` is written only when they're done and `materializePartial` credits the partial streak at phase end (otherwise four correct steps would be worth as much as none). Its per-step reference + next term ride `playerData` alone, while the broadcast carries `dominoProgress` — step counts only, no values, which is what lets the TV show the race. **Every answer reduces to `{score, remaining}`** ([BitkaState.ts](../../packages/server/src/game/games/bitka/BitkaState.ts)) and all three decisions read only those: the land-grab pick queue sorts by score then speed, `resolveScoredDuel` gives the land to the higher score and returns `'tiebreak'` only on a true tie, and `qualifyThreshold` (half the max, rounded up) is the bar for the two spots where there is nobody to compare against — who may pick in the race, and an attack on a neutral territory. For choice and text questions every score is 0 or 1, so all of that is literally the old rule; the graded types slot in with no extra branch — matrica scores 0–3 (cells hit), redosled 0–n (items in the right place), domino 0–n (streak before the first miss) — and because 2:3 is already a decision they **reach the tiebreak less often, so a graded type makes the game shorter**. **The game-select type filter is what makes a new type reachable**: the config popup renders the ordinary kviz pack picker for `osvajanje`, so `host:start-game` has always carried `quizTypes` — but `BitkaModule` ignored it, making the checkboxes a control that did nothing. It honours the filter now (`parseTypeFilter`, intersected with `BITKA_QUIZ_TYPES` and falling back to the unfiltered pool if it empties), the filter applies to the question pool only (the number pool is machinery — "only matrica" must not mean "no tiebreak"), and both pickers (TV and the hostless phone) now offer only the four types the game can place instead of chips for geo/audio/video it discards. This matters more than it sounds: **matrica is ~3% of the packs (31 of 1017 usable questions), so across a ~20-question game the expected count is 0.6** — leave every type checked and a new type simply will not show up. Checking it is how you ask for it. **New types have no fallback, deliberately**: they live in dedicated packs (`matrica.json` is all matrica), so pulling a reserve the way `pogodi-broj` backs the number question would turn "I picked history" into a game of grids nobody asked for — pick a pack without them and the game stays a choice game. A pack with no usable question at all still falls back to the built-in bank. **What is public is the prompt, never the answer**: matrica's nine cells, redosled's *shuffled* items, domino's button labels and step count, the emoji/quote/scramble. The reveal fields (`correctIndex`/`correctCells`/`correctOrder`/`correctText`/`dominoChain`/`explanation`) are reveal-only and the private picks (`selectedCells`/`selectedOrder`/`myText`/`lastWrongText`/`domino`) ride `playerData` — all enforced by the test's leak scanner, where `domino` is checked as a **key** (`"domino":`) because as a bare word it collides with `"kind":"domino"`. Each type renders from one component per package (`BitkaMatricaGrid`/`BitkaNewTypes` (with `BitkaScoreRow` as the one score row) on the TV, `BitkaMatricaCells`/`BitkaNewTypes` on the phone) reused by the answer screen, the reveal, `DuelSpectator` and the hostless strip, and every reveal carries a `score`/max row — with a graded type, "tačno/netačno" hides the very thing that decided the duel. Everything except domino is submitted **once**, on a confirm button: the rule is one answer per question, and separate taps would raise the question of who locked in first. Shuffling (`shuffledItems`, `scramble`) happens in `setQuestion`, not in `buildGameState` — state goes out on every tick, so deriving it there would reshuffle the list under the player's thumb. A question's `imageUrl` *is* honoured on every surface: TV panel (a thumbnail beside the text, plus a smaller one on the tiebreak strip), the answerer's phone screen and the hostless strip, all through `BitkaQuestionImage` / `QuestionImage`, which drop themselves if the image fails to load. **The pack name rides along as the question's category** (`packName` on `BitkaQuestionView`, from a `packNames` map the module keeps because `loadPools` flattens every pack into one pool): gold uppercase above the question text on the answer screens, `DuelSpectator` and the hostless strip. **Phone only**, like the same strip in Kviz — the TV panel already carries the map, the target and the points. The built-in bank has no name and the number question is left without one on purpose: the intro measure and the tiebreak are machinery whose pack the server picks (`pogodi-broj`), so a category there would name something the host never chose. Attack resolution is a **pure function** ([duel.ts](../../packages/server/src/game/games/bitka/duel.ts)) — change outcomes there, not with if-branches in the module. **Castles are placed in turn**, ordered by the intro number question (`baseQueue`): only the active player gets `selectableIds`, each placement is committed immediately and visible to everyone, so later players choose tactically against the castles already on the map — there are no collisions and therefore no re-pick rounds. Both land-grab picks and attack targets are restricted to territories adjacent to what you already hold, falling back to the whole map when nothing adjacent qualifies (`freePickTargets` / `attackTargets`) — so a boxed-in player never loses their turn. **Hostless rooms get a "TV strip"** ([BitkaHostlessPanel.tsx](../../packages/controller/src/games/bitka/components/BitkaHostlessPanel.tsx), rendered only when `room.hostless`): the question and its options for players who aren't answering (a duel spectator otherwise sees nothing), per-player chips for who answered / who is picking, the pick queue, the round context line, the full duel-outcome sentence, and an always-on mini scoreboard. With a TV present none of it renders — that screen already carries it. The event derivation that drives the TV's 3D effects lives in `@igra/shared` ([bitka-fx.ts](../../packages/shared/src/games/bitka-fx.ts)) precisely so the phone can reuse it for a territory flash, CSS flames over a burning territory (`.bitka-fire` — the phone-side counterpart of the TV's three.js fire, since the controller never loads three) and haptics, with both surfaces speaking one event vocabulary and one timing plan (`planFxTiming`). Server state carries the *new* owner immediately while effects are deliberately delayed, so both surfaces render a **deferred board** (`planFxHolds` + a `shownBoard` state, never `host.board` directly) — the map lags exactly long enough for the impact to land before the colour turns; deriving that in an effect alone would flash the new colour for one frame, since passive effects run after paint. **The reveal happens on the question screen, not the map screen**: `*-rezultat` keeps the question and its options up and stamps each player's avatar onto the option they picked, with the correct one green and checked (TV panel, the answerer's phone screen, and the hostless strip all render it the same way from `hostData.results`, which already carries per-player `optionIndex`). **A duel reveals in two steps before anything touches the board**: `duel-odgovor-rezultat` holds the choice question up with the correct option and both avatars, and a drawn duel then gets `duel-broj-rezultat` for the number — correct value, both guesses closest-first, and each duellist's confirm time, since speed is what splits equal guesses. Then comes **`duel-ishod`, a window that reports the outcome while the board is still untouched** — "Porodin menja gospodara", "jedan zid manje" — on both surfaces, for `DUEL_ISHOD_DURATION` (4 s). That one exists because the FX derive from a board diff: `applyDuel(room, win, commit)` runs **twice**, first with `commit: false` (compute the outcome, write `lastEvent` / `lastOutcome`, touch nothing else) and then, when the window closes, with `commit: true` (owners, walls, bonuses, scores) — which is the moment `deriveFxEvents` sees a changed board and the weapon flies. The preview pass must **not** publish `duel.outcome` (FX key off its appearance); it sends `pendingOutcome` + `wallsAfter` instead, and the `zid` sentence computes the remaining wall count rather than reading it off the board, which is still one higher. Both reveal steps share one tunable knob, `DUEL_OTKRIVANJE_DURATION` (3 s); the window has its own. Note what this costs: a duel is ~7 s longer, ~10 s through a tiebreak — turn those knobs down in /admin → Timinzi if the war drags. **Every question reveals, including the ones that only decide a tiebreak**: the intro number question reveals on its own screen (`redosled-rezultat` keeps the broj question up with the correct value and everyone's guess, closest first), and a drawn duel reveals its number on `duel-broj-rezultat`. **Each question reveals exactly once, on its own screen** — `duel-rezultat` is deliberately *not* a reveal phase (`showQuestion` / `revealing` in `buildGameState` both exclude it), so the server sends no question, no `correctIndex` and no `results` there and the TV panel, the phone and the hostless strip all fall back to the outcome sentence alone. Re-showing it over the map meant reading the same answer a third time on top of the one screen that has something else to show. That also retired the whole two-questions-on-one-screen apparatus: `hostData.tiebreak`, `BitkaTiebreakView`, the host's `TiebreakStrip`, the controller's `BitkaTiebreak`, the `choiceResults` snapshot and `DUEL_TIEBREAK_EXTRA` are gone, because `state.answers` may now be overwritten by the tiebreak — the choice round was already shown. **A duel is announced inside the countdown, not before it**: entering `duel-pitanje` puts up one Objava that names who attacks whom, for which territory, its walls and its points, while its circle counts down to the question — announcement and counter are ONE element, so the phase lasts just `PITANJE_NAJAVA_DURATION` (the earlier separate announcement card and its `BITKA_NAJAVA_MS` extra beat are gone; a duel is ~2.5 s shorter, and both TV and phone show the card the whole phase off the same server clock). A siege turns the whole Objava red (`--danger`, pulsing) and carries the wall state — the only attack that can eliminate a player. **A continued siege gets no full announcement** (`opsadaNastavak` on the duel view, from `beginDuel(..., continuation)`): same attacker, same castle, so it would repeat up to three times inside one attack — its Objava keeps only the counting circle and the "opsada se nastavlja" line. The TV dims the board under it rather than blurring it — `backdrop-filter` over the WebGL canvas can snapshot black, which turns the board into a hole. **`*-pitanje` is a countdown, not a preview**: the server does not put the question in the broadcast at all during `redosled-pitanje` / `osvajanje-pitanje` / `duel-pitanje` (`showQuestion` in `buildGameState`), and both surfaces draw the translucent central **"Objava"** over the board instead — the one info element every in-game message uses (left circle with the countdown number or an outcome glyph, gold uppercase overline, one-sentence title, sub-line; plain `rgba` dim, never `backdrop-filter` over the WebGL canvas). During `osvajanje-pitanje` its title carries the consequence of the previous move (`lastEvent` — "Pera uzima Porodin"): the countdown and "what just happened" share the beat, so they share the element. The same Objava carries the pick phases (`baza-izbor` / `osvajanje-izbor` / `napad-izbor`: who picks, the queue and the remaining time — the header clock and the bottom strip hide while it stands, so one sentence appears in exactly one place per frame) and the no-winner reveal (`osvajanje-rezultat` with no correct answer: grey border, 🚩 circle, the correct answer in the sub-line instead of the option grid); `duel-ishod` renders as its glyph variant with the dim raised to 0.58. Before this the question text appeared beside the map the moment the phase started and then again full-screen seconds later, so the same question was read twice in two places; now the pause only says *when* to look up, and everyone opens the question in the same instant. Nothing but the counter may go there — put the question back and the leak is back. **The phone's status line is a centred mini-Objava** (`StatusCard`): who is picking, who just took what — and in the pick phases it also carries the overline and the countdown circle, so the sentence and the clock live in one place (the hostless strip drops its duplicate title there and keeps only the queue and the rule). It used to be small text at the top edge over a colourful map, which is where it went unread. It is suppressed in `duel-rezultat` — that screen belongs to the capture animation and nothing may sit on top of it. **`duel-odgovor` / `duel-broj` get their own TV layout** (`duelArena` in BitkaHost): map, target card and a one-line standings table squeeze into a left column, and the question takes the rest of the screen with a slot per duellist reading `zaključao ✓` / `razmišlja…`. Those two slots come straight from `attackerCommitted` / `defenderCommitted` — the two booleans the server sends precisely because *what* was picked may not enter the broadcast. The header clock doubles in size there and the whole header keeps right padding, since the host's `Završi igru` button owns that corner. **Everyone who isn't in the duel gets the same question on their phone, read-only** (`DuelSpectator`): plain tiles instead of buttons, the two lock-in chips, and the countdown — and it stays through the two reveal steps, which is a spectator's only chance to see what the answer was. The question is already in the broadcast half, so nothing new is revealed; this is the one place where the TV-mode phone deliberately does show the question, because a duel otherwise leaves half the table looking at a map with no idea what is being asked. It ends with the duel — the outcome is still watched on the map. **The outcome lands with a weapon**: `deriveFxEvents` emits `mac` (attack went through) or `stit` (defence held) next to the existing events, and everything that follows from the hit — the wall going out, the colour turning — waits `FX_SLAM_SECONDS` for it to land, on top of the projectile's flight. All three surfaces draw the same beat from the same event: the 3D board and the flat FX canvas build it from primitives (blade, guard, grip, pommel / disc, rim, boss), the phone slams the ⚔️/🛡️ glyph over the territory in CSS (`.bitka-slam`). **On the phone `duel-rezultat` is the one reveal that stays on the map** (`duelReveal` in the controller — `osvajanje-rezultat` still gets the full-screen option grid): the payoff of a duel *is* the map — wall burning, land changing hands, castle falling — and it fires the instant the phase flips, so a full-screen question hides exactly what the player just won (the FX only replays later when the map returns, or never, if that hit ended the game). Duellists get the map plus a compact `Reveal` line / hostless strip; the full reveal is the TV's job, per the usual rule that a phone doesn't duplicate a TV in the room. Related: [BitkaMapPicker.tsx](../../packages/controller/src/games/bitka/components/BitkaMapPicker.tsx) seeds its played-FX cursor from whatever events exist **at mount**, because the map is unmounted and remounted all game (question, slider) and would otherwise re-flash the previous turn's hit on every return. Without all this the map simply changed colour with no visible reason. Only then does `osvajanje-izbor` come up, and it is deliberately bare — no question, no answer — because that screen is about the map. An earlier version merged the two (pick immediately, answer shown alongside) and was reverted: the reveal is worth its own beat, and mixing it into the pick screen buried who answered what. On the phone the map takes the **whole screen** (a fixed, full-bleed stage that breaks out of `#root`'s padding; titles and hints float over it), so the target list and the score table both live in the player popup instead ([BitkaBoardMenu.tsx](../../packages/controller/src/components/BitkaBoardMenu.tsx), rendered by `PlayerMenu`). Unlike the other games it renders in TV rooms too — a player who isn't on turn otherwise has no idea where anything is happening. **TV-only 3D board + FX**: on the TV the map is a three.js scene ([fx/](../../packages/host/src/games/bitka/fx/)) — each territory is its own extruded slab, raised when focused, with castles/walls as primitives (the castle of whoever is on turn scales up and pulses); the camera pushes in on the duelled territory and pulls back out. Territory names are **not** drawn on the TV (the panel names them in prose), and the 3D canvas is deliberately not aspect-locked to the map image — the camera frames the board into whatever box the layout gives it, by bisecting for the closest distance at which all eight board corners still project inside the frustum. That corner check matters: the camera is tilted, so the near edge enters the frame far more narrowly than a flat "fit the depth into the height" estimate suggests — that estimate silently clipped the near corners while leaving a large empty band above the far edge. **Any uploaded map works with zero extra content**, because `ExtrudeGeometry` derives UVs straight from the shape coordinates, so a polygon drawn in the stored `[0,1]` space picks up exactly its own slice of the PNG; a darkened full-image plane sits under the slabs so the parts of the illustration that aren't territories stay visible. `?board=2d` on the host URL falls back to the flat map with a transparent FX canvas over it, which is also the automatic fallback when WebGL is missing and the `Suspense` placeholder while the three chunk loads. Both surfaces animate attacks, defences, captures, wall hits, castle falls and the win. Events are derived by a **pure diff of two consecutive `BitkaHostData` snapshots** ([bitka-fx.ts](../../packages/shared/src/games/bitka-fx.ts)) — no new socket events, no server involvement, each event fires exactly once, and a repeated state (reconnect/replay) produces nothing. The scene ([BitkaFxScene.ts](../../packages/host/src/games/bitka/fx/BitkaFxScene.ts)) follows the Penali rules: vanilla three, no assets, primitives only, and it lives behind a `lazy()` boundary so three lands in its own chunk (`BitkaFx-*.js`) — the main bundle and even the `BitkaHost` chunk stay free of it. Its ortho camera is the normalized `[0,1]` map space, so FX coordinates are the same ones the polygons use. **Effects are decorative and must stay short** (the weapon is the longest at `FX_SLAM_SECONDS` 1.45 s of fall + 0.5 s hold ≈ 2 s, against the 3 s minimum of `DUEL_REZULTAT_DURATION`; a castle-fall cascade can exceed that and simply gets cut). `FX_SLAM_SECONDS` is the shared clock for both surfaces, so the phone's `.bitka-slam` / `-flash` / `-ring` keyframes carry hand-computed percentages that put the impact at the same absolute moment (72 % of 2 s) plus a matching `SLAM_MS` — change the constant and those three percentages move with it, or TV and phone land the blow in different beats: the server clock never waits for the TV, and `clear()` drops everything mid-flight. Phones never load any of this — the controller package has no three dependency, which is what keeps hostless simple; that layer is the intended seam for a later extruded-3D board. The map's strict check (≥9 territories, connected neighbour graph, image present) is what gates `visibleInGame`; a half-drawn map stays editable but never reaches game-select. Headless end-to-end + anti-leak run: `npx tsx scripts/test-bitka.mts` (`--players 2..4`, `--rounds n`, `--map id`, `--pack a,b` / `--pack all`, `--types a,b`, `--full`). **The default run is the fast one**: it skips the text types (emoji/dopuna/anagram — every other bot deliberately stays silent on them so the phase runs the full 20 s) and uses minimal wait timings, ~2–3 min per game; `--full` puts the text types back, which is the only run that exercises the timeout path and the `myText`/`lastWrongText` leak checks. A two-player game legitimately ends in war round 1 when the bots chain three siege wins — that is the documented siege rule, not a round-counting bug. **Without `--pack` the game reads nothing off disk** — it falls back to the built-in bank, which has no matrica, so a bare `--types matrica` proves nothing; `--pack all` is what the TV sends when the host doesn't touch the pack selection. `--pack matrica` also exercises the `pogodi-broj` tiebreak fallback, that pack carrying no number question.
